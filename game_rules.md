# Chronicles of the Fall

## Official Prototype Rulebook

A semi-cooperative game of imperial decline for 3–5 players.

---

# 1. Game Overview

In **Chronicles of the Fall**, players are members of a ruling council guiding a fragile Empire through expansion, crisis, corruption, disease, unrest, and eventual collapse.

The Empire does not win. The Empire will fall.

Each player secretly represents a faction with a Hidden Agenda. Players must help the Empire survive long enough to shape it, but they are not trying to preserve the same future. When the Empire collapses, Hidden Agendas are revealed. A player wins if the final state of the Empire satisfies their Agenda.

The central mechanic of the game is the **Anonymous Council**. Each Era, players secretly commit cards into the machinery of government. The cards are revealed anonymously into the Council Docket, ordered by the Minister of the Empire, and then resolved one at a time. Some cards build the Empire. Others disturb it. Since most contributors are anonymous, players must infer who is shaping the Empire, who is protecting it, and who is accelerating its fall.

---

# 2. Objective

The game ends immediately when any of the three Imperial Pillars reaches 0:

* **Treasury**
* **Stability**
* **Morale**

When the game ends, all players reveal their Hidden Agendas.

A player wins if their Hidden Agenda condition is satisfied at the moment of collapse.

If multiple players satisfy their Agenda, the Agenda cards determine tie-breakers. If no Agenda-specific tie-breaker is available, the tied player with the most cards remaining in hand and Scheme Slots wins. If still tied, those players share the victory.

If no player satisfies their Hidden Agenda, all players lose.

---

# 3. Core Concepts

## 3.1 The Empire

The Empire is shared by all players.

It consists of:

* Cities
* Structures
* Global Resources
* Permanent Tags
* Condition Tokens
* Three Pillars: Treasury, Stability, Morale

No player owns a City or Structure unless a card explicitly says otherwise.

---

## 3.2 Pillars

The three Pillars measure the survival of the Empire.

| Pillar    | Meaning                                                         |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treasury  | Money, taxation, reserves, economic legitimacy                  |
| Stability | political order, administrative control, institutional cohesion |
| Morale    | public hope, loyalty, social confidence                         |

Each Pillar starts at 5.

If any Pillar reaches 0, the game ends immediately after resolving the effect that caused the reduction.

---

## 3.3 Tags

Tags are permanent qualities of the Empire.

Examples include:

* Military
* Sanitary
* Food
* Industry
* Commerce
* Faith
* Knowledge
* Authority
* Bureaucracy
* Urban
* Frontier

Tags are not resources. They are never spent.

Tags are used to:

* satisfy card requirements;
* resolve Event effects;
* determine Hidden Agenda conditions;
* protect against some Crisis Events;
* define the identity of Cities and Structures.

Unless a card says otherwise, a Structure provides its tags as long as it remains built.

---

## 3.4 Resources

Resources are temporary productive capacity generated by the Empire each Era.

Common resources:

* Labor
* Material
* Wealth

Rare resources:

* Arms
* Medicine
* Faith
* Knowledge
* Authority

Resources are global. No player owns them.

Resources are spent to build Development cards or resolve Event cards.

Unused resources are discarded at the end of each Era unless stored by Cities.

---

# 4. Card Types

There are two main card categories:

1. **Development cards**
2. **Event cards**

---

## 4.1 Development Cards

Development cards permanently change the Empire.

There are two Development subtypes:

### City

City cards create new City zones.

A City may have:

* tags;
* building slots;
* production;
* storage;
* special rules.

Cities do not occupy building slots.

Example:

**Frontier Town**
Type: Development — City
Cost: 1 Labor, 1 Material
Tags: Frontier
Slots: 3
Production: +1 Labor
Storage: 1

---

### Structure

Structure cards are built into Cities.

A Structure may have:

* tag requirements;
* resource cost;
* tags provided;
* production;
* built effects;
* ongoing effects;
* storage bonuses.

A Structure occupies one open slot in a City.

Unless marked as a **Global Requirement**, a Structure’s tag requirements must be satisfied by the City where it is placed.

Example:

**Public Baths**
Type: Development — Structure
Requirement: Urban
Cost: 1 Labor, 1 Material, 1 Wealth
Provides: Sanitary
Built Effect: +1 Morale

This Structure can only be built in a City with the Urban tag.

---

## 4.2 Event Cards

Event cards resolve immediately when revealed.

There are three Event subtypes:

### Incident

Incidents are immediate disturbances or opportunities.

They may add or remove resources, place Condition Tokens, modify Pillars, discard cards, or trigger local effects.

A resource effect has a signed amount. A positive amount adds resources to the
Global Resource Pool and a negative amount removes them, without reducing a
resource below zero. The effect may name a specific resource. If it is general,
the Minister of Health & Harvest chooses the affected resource; if that Ministry
is vacant, the Minister of the Empire chooses.

An Event may name an optional **Choice Minister**. If present, that Minister
handles every choice created by that Event instead of the normal effect-specific
Minister. If the named Ministry is vacant, the Minister of the Empire decides.
If no Choice Minister is named, apply the normal decision rules for each effect.

A conditional effect may compare a tag, resource, or Pillar value with a fixed
number. A tag condition may instead compare its count with the current count of
another tag, using the same greater-than, less-than, at-least, at-most, or equal
operators.

An Event may grant a **Structure Requirement Waiver** for the current Era. The
next Structure built that Era may be missing at most one required tag instance
in its destination City. The waiver is consumed automatically when that
Structure is built, even if all of its tag requirements were already satisfied.
It cannot waive resource costs, placement rules, or more than one missing tag.
An unused waiver expires at the end of the Era.

An Event may convert up to X units of one volatile resource into the same number
of another volatile resource. It converts the smaller of X and the amount
currently available. The source, destination, or both may be general. General
resource types are chosen by the Event's Choice Minister, or by the Minister of
Health & Harvest under the normal rules. Source and destination must differ.

An Event may make its Choice Minister draw one card from the Empire Deck. If the
Event has no Choice Minister, or the named Ministry is vacant, the Minister of
the Empire draws instead.

Example:

**Ratborne Fever**
Type: Event — Incident
Effect: Place 1 Plague on a City chosen by the Minister of Health & Harvest.

---

### Edict

Edicts are political decisions, reforms, emergency laws, or administrative maneuvers.

They may require resources and often change Pillars or modify rules temporarily.

Example:

**Emergency Taxation**
Type: Event — Edict
Cost: 1 Authority
Effect: Add 2 Wealth to the Global Resource Pool. Lose 1 Morale.

---

### Crisis

Crisis Events are severe player-triggered disasters or confrontations.

A Crisis usually checks tags, tokens, or Pillars, then applies a success or failure effect.

Example:

**Border Raid**
Type: Event — Crisis
Check: Military 2
Success: Add 1 Wealth next Era.
Failure: Lose 1 Stability.

There is no separate Crisis Deck. Crisis Events are played by players from hand or Scheme Slots like any other card.

---

# 6. Setup

## 6.1 Set Pillars

Set the Pillars to:

* Treasury: 5
* Stability: 5
* Morale: 5

---

## 6.2 Place the Capital

Place the Capital in play.

**Capital**
Type: City
Tags: Urban, Bureaucracy
Slots: 4
Production: 2 Labor, 1 Material, 1 Wealth
Storage: 2

---

## 6.3 Prepare the Empire Deck

Shuffle the Empire Deck.

The Empire Deck contains Development cards and Event cards.

Recommended prototype deck composition:

* 40–45% Development cards
* 40–45% Event cards
* 10–20% flexible political, crisis, or advanced cards

---

## 6.4 Deal Hidden Agendas

Deal 1 Hidden Agenda to each player.

Players keep their Agenda secret until the Empire collapses.

---

## 6.5 Deal Starting Hands

Create a Base Card Pool appropriate for the player count.

Each player receives:

* 3 random Base cards
* 2 random cards from the Empire Deck

Each player therefore starts with 5 cards.

Suggested Base cards include:

**Farm**

Cost: 1 Labor
Provides: Food
Production: +1 Labor

**Quarry**
Type: Development — Structure
Cost: 1 Labor
Provides: Industry
Production: +2 Material

**Garrison**
Type: Development — Structure
Cost: 1 Material
Provides: Military
Production: +1 Arms

**Market**
Type: Development — Structure
Cost: 1 Labor, 1 Material
Provides: Commerce
Production: +1 Wealth

**Shrine**
Type: Development — Structure
Cost: 1 Material
Provides: Faith
Production: +1 Faith

**Clinic**
Type: Development — Structure

Provides: Sanitary
Production: +1 Medicine

---

## 6.6 Scheme Slots

Each player has 2 private Scheme Slots.

Players may store cards in Scheme Slots during Cleanup.

Cards in Scheme Slots:

* are private;
* do not count against hand limit;
* may be played during the Plotting Phase;
* may be swapped with cards from hand during Cleanup.

---

## 6.7 Hand Limit

The hand limit is 5.

If a player has more than 5 cards in hand after drawing and scheming, they must discard down to 5.

Cards in Scheme Slots do not count toward the hand limit.

---

# 7. Ministries and the Minister of the Empire

## 7.1 Minister of the Empire

The Minister of the Empire is the Regent, the first player of the Era, and the
default decision-maker when another Ministry is vacant.

During the Council Docket Phase, the Minister of the Empire determines the order
in which all committed cards resolve.

The Minister of the Empire cannot receive Suspicion and cannot be blocked or
otherwise prevented from holding this office. An effect that would block that
player may still prevent them from selecting another Ministry, but it cannot
remove or skip their position as Minister of the Empire.

At setup, choose a random player to become Minister of the Empire.

At the beginning of each new Era after the first, pass the office clockwise.

---

## 7.2 Ministries

There are four Ministries:

1. Minister of Cities
2. Minister of State
3. Minister of Health & Harvest
4. Minister of War

Ministries are assigned randomly at setup.

At the beginning of each new Era after the first, all Ministry cards rotate clockwise.

There is no Ministry draft.

---

## 7.3 Ministry Assignment by Player Count

### 3 Players

Use the four Ministry cards and one vacant placeholder position.

Randomly assign the four Ministry cards among the three players and the vacant placeholder.

Each Era, all Ministry cards rotate clockwise through the three players and the vacant placeholder.

A Ministry on the vacant placeholder is inactive that Era.

### 4 Players

Randomly assign one Ministry to each player.

Each Era, all Ministry cards rotate clockwise.

### 5 Players

Use the four Ministry cards and one **No Ministry** marker.

Randomly assign the four Ministry cards and the No Ministry marker among the five players.

Each Era, all five status cards rotate clockwise.

The player with the No Ministry marker has no Ministry that Era.

---

## 7.4 Vacant Ministries

A Ministry may be vacant because:

* it is assigned to the vacant placeholder in a 3-player game;
* it is lost due to Suspicion;
* a card effect disables it;
* no player currently holds it.

If a vacant Ministry must make a decision, the Minister of the Empire makes that decision instead.

---

# 8. Ministry Powers

## 8.1 Minister of Cities

The Minister of Cities places all Cities and Structures.

When a Development card is successfully built, the Minister of Cities chooses its legal placement.

The Minister of Cities also chooses which leftover resources are stored if the Empire has more resources than storage capacity.

If an effect destroys, moves, or targets a Structure and no other Minister is named, the Minister of Cities chooses the target.

---

## 8.2 Minister of State

The Minister of State governs imperial legitimacy and political disorder.

When an effect places Unrest and does not specify whether it is City Unrest or Global Unrest, the Minister of State chooses.

If City Unrest is chosen, the Minister of State also chooses the City.

During Cleanup, the Minister of State draws up to 2 cards instead of 1, without exceeding the hand limit.

---

## 8.3 Minister of Health & Harvest

The Minister of Health & Harvest governs disease placement and food-health administration.

When an effect places Plague and does not specify a City, the Minister of Health & Harvest chooses the City.

The Minister of Health & Harvest cannot be forced to discard cards from hand or Scheme Slots.

If an effect forces all players to discard, the Minister of Health & Harvest ignores that discard.

If an effect specifically targets the Minister of Health & Harvest with forced discard, the discard is ignored.

This protection applies only to forced discard. The Minister may still voluntarily discard cards to pay costs or use effects.

---

## 8.4 Minister of War

The Minister of War resolves revolts.

When Global Unrest reaches its threshold, the Minister of State chooses how the Imperial Unrest Crisis is resolved.

When a City reaches 2 Unrest, the Minister of War chooses how that revolt is resolved.

If a revolt destroys Structures, the Minister of War chooses which Structures are destroyed.

When an effect places Fortified, the Minister of War chooses the city.

---

# 9. Era Structure

Each Era follows this sequence:

1. Rotate the Minister of the Empire and Ministries
2. Suspicion Phase
3. Production Phase
4. Queued Project Resolution
5. Plotting Phase
6. Council Docket Phase
7. Stalled Project Vote
8. Condition Phase
9. Storage Phase
10. Scheme / Draw / Cleanup

During the first Era, do not rotate the Minister of the Empire or Ministries after setup. Begin with Suspicion.

---

# 10. Suspicion Phase

Suspicion represents public blame, court rumors, and political distrust.

At the beginning of each Era, after the Minister of the Empire and Ministries are set, each player places one Suspicion token.

Starting with the Minister of the Empire and proceeding clockwise, each player places their Suspicion token on:

* another player;
* or no one.

A player may not place Suspicion on themselves.

A player may not place Suspicion on the Minister of the Empire.

Suspicion tokens are public.

---

## 10.1 Suspicion Effects

After all Suspicion tokens are placed, count how many each player received.

### 0–1 Suspicion

No effect.

### 2+ Suspicion

The player suffers both effects for this Era:

1. They lose their Ministry for this Era, if they have one.
2. They must commit their card face-up during the Plotting Phase.

A lost Ministry becomes vacant until the end of the Era. If that Ministry must make a decision, the Minister of the Empire decides instead.

The player still participates normally in all other parts of the Era.

---

## 10.2 Clearing Suspicion

All Suspicion tokens are removed at the end of each Era.

Suspicion does not persist unless a card explicitly says otherwise.

---

# 11. Production Phase

During Production, the Empire produces resources.

Add to the Global Resource Pool:

* production from all Cities;
* production from all Structures;
* stored resources from previous Eras.

Stored resources are moved from Cities into the Global Resource Pool during Production.

Resources in the Global Resource Pool are shared by the Empire and owned by no player.

Example:

The Empire has:

* Capital: 2 Labor, 1 Material, 1 Wealth
* Quarry: +2 Material
* Market: +1 Wealth

The Global Resource Pool becomes:

* 2 Labor
* 3 Material
* 2 Wealth

---

# 12. Queued Project Resolution

Some Stalled Projects may have been voted to continue from the previous Era. These are called Queued Projects.

After Production and before players commit new cards, resolve Queued Projects from left to right.

For each Queued Project:

* if all requirements are satisfied and all required resources are available, build it and spend the resources;
* if even one requirement is missing, one legal placement is missing, or one required resource is unavailable, move it to the Stalled Row for the current Era.

Queued Projects that fail do not automatically remain queued. They must survive the Stalled Project Vote again.

---

# 13. Plotting Phase

Each player must commit one card if able.

A player may commit:

* one card from hand;
* or one card from a Scheme Slot.

Cards are normally committed face-down.

A player with 2+ Suspicion must commit face-up.

---

## 13.1 Anonymous Commitments

All committed cards are collected without revealing who contributed each
face-down card.

Players should not know which player submitted which face-down card.

---

## 13.2 Face-Up Committed Cards

If a player must commit face-up, the card remains visibly associated with that player.

For physical play:

* place the real card face-up in front of that player;
* place a face-down placeholder among the commitments;
* when commitments enter the Council Docket, replace that placeholder with the
  associated face-up card.

For digital play, the Docket shows that the card belongs to the suspected player.

---

# 14. Council Docket Phase

After every player has committed or declared that they cannot commit:

1. Reveal all committed cards into the **Council Docket**.
2. Keep each normal contributor anonymous. A card committed face-up because of
   Suspicion remains associated with its contributor.
3. The Minister of the Empire orders every card in the Docket.
4. Lock the chosen order.
5. Resolve the first card, then continue through the Docket from left to right.

The Docket is not shuffled and its resolution order is not random.

Resolve each card fully before resolving the next card.

If any effect changes the Global Resource Pool, that change applies immediately
and may affect cards later in the Docket.

If any effect causes a Pillar to reach 0, finish resolving that card, then the game ends immediately.

---

# 15. Resolving Development Cards

When a Development card is revealed, check whether it can be built.

A Development card can be built only if:

1. all tag requirements are satisfied;
2. all resource costs are available in the Global Resource Pool;
3. a legal City or placement target exists;
4. a Structure has an available slot in the chosen City.

If all conditions are satisfied, the card is built.

If even one condition is not satisfied, the card is not built and enters the Stalled Row.

---

## 15.1 Resource Consumption

Resource payment is all-or-nothing.

If the full cost is available, spend the full cost and build the card.

If even one required resource is missing, spend nothing and place the card in the Stalled Row.

Example:

**Academy**
Requirement: Urban, Knowledge
Cost: 2 Wealth
Provides: Knowledge, Bureaucracy
Production: +1 Knowledge

If the Empire has Urban and 2 Wealth but no Knowledge tag, Academy cannot be built. No Wealth is spent. Academy enters the Stalled Row.

---

## 15.2 Structure Placement

When a Structure is successfully built, the Minister of Cities chooses the City where it is placed.

The chosen City must:

* satisfy the Structure’s requirements;
* have an open slot;
* obey all card-specific placement rules.

If no legal City exists, the Structure enters the Stalled Row.

---

## 15.3 City Placement

When a City card is successfully built, the Minister of Cities places it according to the card text.

If the card does not specify restrictions, the City simply enters play as a new City zone.

---

# 16. Resolving Event Cards

Event cards resolve immediately when revealed.

Events do not enter the Stalled Row.

If an Event has a cost and the cost cannot be paid, the Event has no effect and is discarded unless the card says otherwise.

If an Event has a condition and the condition is not met, follow the card text. If no alternate effect is listed, discard it with no effect.

Examples:

**Emergency Taxation**
Type: Event — Edict
Cost: 1 Authority
Effect: Add 2 Wealth. Lose 1 Morale.

If the Empire has no Authority, Emergency Taxation is discarded with no effect.

---

**Border Raid**
Type: Event — Crisis
Check: Military 2
Success: Add 1 Wealth next Era.
Failure: Lose 1 Stability.

When revealed, count the Empire’s Military tags. If the Empire has at least 2 Military, resolve the Success effect. Otherwise, resolve the Failure effect.

---

# 17. Stalled Projects

Any Development card that cannot be built enters the Stalled Row.

Cards enter the Stalled Row face-up in the order they failed.

Events never enter the Stalled Row.

---

## 17.1 Stalled Project Vote

After all cards in the Council Docket have been resolved, players vote on which Stalled Projects should remain possible.

Each player has 1 vote.

A player may vote for:

* one Stalled Project;
* or none.

Votes are public.

Any Stalled Project with at least 2 votes becomes a Queued Project for the next Era.

All other Stalled Projects are discarded.

Queued Projects keep their relative order from the Stalled Row.

---

# 18. Condition Tokens

Condition Tokens represent persistent local or global states affecting the Empire.

The prototype uses three Condition Tokens:

* Plague
* Unrest
* Fortified

Plague and Unrest are negative tokens.

Fortified is a positive token.

---

# 19. Plague

Plague represents disease, contamination, famine spillover, or public health collapse.

Plague is placed on Cities.

When an effect places Plague and does not specify a City, the Minister of Health & Harvest chooses the City.

---

## 19.1 Plague Check

During the Condition Phase, check each City separately.

If a City has more Plague tokens than Sanitary tags, lose 1 Morale.

Then leave the Plague tokens in place.

Example:

The Capital has:

* 2 Plague tokens
* 1 Sanitary tag

Because Plague is greater than Sanitary, the Empire loses 1 Morale.

If the Capital later has:

* 2 Plague tokens
* 2 Sanitary tags

there is no Morale loss from Plague in that City.

---

## 19.2 Removing Plague

Cards may remove Plague.

Example:

**Clinic**
Built Effect: Remove up to 1 Plague from this City.

**Hospital**
Built Effect: Remove up to 2 Plague from this City.

**Quarantine Edict**
Type: Event — Edict
Effect: Remove up to 2 Plague from one City. Then place 1 Unrest on that City.

---

# 20. Unrest

Unrest represents revolt, political disorder, social anger, and collapse of legitimacy.

Unrest may be placed:

* on a City;
* or globally on the Empire.

When an effect places Unrest and does not specify City or Global, the Minister of State chooses whether to place it globally or on a City.

If City Unrest is chosen and the effect does not specify a City, the Minister of State chooses the City.

---

## 20.1 City Unrest

City Unrest is placed on Cities.

When a City reaches 2 Unrest tokens, a Revolt immediately triggers in that City.

The Minister of War chooses one resolution:

### Suppress the Revolt

Remove all Unrest from that City.
Lose 1 Morale.

### Buy Peace

Remove all Unrest from that City.
Lose 1 Treasury.

### Let It Burn

Remove all Unrest from that City.
Destroy 2 Structures in that City, chosen by the Minister of War.

If fewer than 2 Structures are present, destroy as many as possible.

Cities themselves are not destroyed by Revolt unless a card says otherwise.

---

## 20.2 Global Unrest

Global Unrest is placed on the Empire.

When the Empire reaches 3 Global Unrest tokens, an Imperial Unrest Crisis immediately triggers.

The Minister of State chooses one resolution:

### Repression

Remove all Global Unrest.
Lose 2 Morale.
Gain 1 Stability.

### Concessions

Remove all Global Unrest.
Lose 2 Treasury.
Gain 1 Morale.

### Fragmentation

Remove all Global Unrest.
Lose 2 Stability.
Gain 1 Treasury.

---

## 20.3 Timing of Unrest

Unrest thresholds resolve immediately.

If an Event places the second Unrest on a City, resolve the City Revolt before continuing with the Event if possible.

If an Event places the third Global Unrest token, resolve the Imperial Unrest Crisis immediately before revealing the next card.

---

# 21. Fortified

Fortified represents walls, guard networks, military readiness, or protected infrastructure.

Fortified is placed on Cities.

A City may have at most 1 Fortified token.

A Fortified City contributes +1 Military tag.

If a Structure in a Fortified City would be destroyed, remove the Fortified token instead of destroying one Structure.

If an effect would destroy 2 Structures in a Fortified City, Fortified prevents one destruction. The other destruction still occurs if a valid Structure remains.

Example:

A City Revolt resolves with Let It Burn in a Fortified City containing three Structures.

The Minister of War chooses two Structures to destroy.

Fortified is removed to prevent one of those destructions.

The other chosen Structure is destroyed.

---

# 22. Condition Phase

During the Condition Phase:

1. Resolve all Plague checks.
2. Apply any other “during Condition Phase” effects.

Unrest does not wait until the Condition Phase. Unrest resolves immediately when its threshold is reached.

Fortified has no Condition Phase effect.

---

# 23. Storage Phase

After the Condition Phase, unused resources in the Global Resource Pool are discarded unless stored.

Each City has storage capacity.

The Capital has Storage 2 by default.

Other Cities and Structures may add storage.

If there are more leftover resources than available storage, the Minister of Cities chooses which resources are stored.

Stored resources are placed on Cities.

During the next Production Phase, all stored resources are moved into the Global Resource Pool.

Example:

The Empire has leftover:

* 1 Labor
* 1 Material
* 2 Wealth

The Capital has Storage 2.

The Minister of Cities chooses to store:

* 1 Material
* 1 Wealth

The remaining Labor and Wealth are discarded.

---

# 24. Scheme / Draw / Cleanup

At the end of each Era, each player may manage Scheme Slots.

A player may do one of the following:

* place one card from hand into an empty Scheme Slot;
* swap one card from hand with one card in a Scheme Slot;
* do nothing.

Then each player draws 1 card.

The Minister of State draws up to 2 cards instead of 1, without exceeding the hand limit.

After drawing, each player discards down to the hand limit of 5.

Cards discarded from hand or Scheme Slots go to the discard pile.

Resolved Events go to the discard pile.

Development cards that are built remain in play.

Destroyed Structures go to the discard pile unless a card says otherwise.

When the Empire Deck is empty, shuffle the discard pile to form a new Empire Deck.

---

# 25. Example Era

## Situation

Four players are playing.

The Empire has:

**Capital**
Tags: Urban, Bureaucracy
Slots: 4
Production: 2 Labor, 1 Material, 1 Wealth
Storage: 2

**Quarry**
Provides: Industry
Production: +2 Material

Pillars:

* Treasury: 5
* Stability: 5
* Morale: 4

The current Ministries are:

* Player A: Minister of Cities
* Player B: Minister of State
* Player C: Minister of Health & Harvest
* Player D: Minister of War

Player B receives 2 Suspicion during the Suspicion Phase.

Because Player B has 2+ Suspicion:

* Player B loses the Ministry of State for this Era;
* Player B must commit their card face-up.

The Ministry of State is vacant this Era. If the Minister of State would make a decision, the Minister of the Empire decides instead.

---

## Production

The Empire produces:

* Capital: 2 Labor, 1 Material, 1 Wealth
* Quarry: +2 Material

Global Resource Pool:

* 2 Labor
* 3 Material
* 1 Wealth

---

## Plotting

Each player commits one card.

Player B must commit face-up because of Suspicion.

All committed cards are revealed into the Council Docket. Player B remains
associated with their card; the other contributors remain anonymous. The
Minister of the Empire orders the Docket before the first card resolves.

---

## Reveal 1

The first card is:

**Tax Riots**
Type: Event — Incident
Effect: Add 2 Wealth. Then, if Morale is 4 or lower, place 1 Unrest.

Morale is 4, so the Unrest is placed.

The Minister of State is vacant, so the Minister of the Empire chooses whether the Unrest is Global or City Unrest.

The Minister of the Empire places it on the Capital.

Global Resource Pool becomes:

* 2 Labor
* 3 Material
* 3 Wealth

The Capital now has 1 Unrest.

---

## Reveal 2

The second card is:

**Garrison**
Type: Development — Structure
Cost: 1 Material
Provides: Military
Production: +1 Arms

The Empire has enough Material.

The Garrison is built.

The Minister of Cities places it in the Capital.

Global Resource Pool becomes:

* 2 Labor
* 2 Material
* 3 Wealth

---

## Reveal 3

The third card is Player B’s face-up card:

**Public Baths**
Type: Development — Structure
Requirement: Urban
Cost: 1 Labor, 1 Material, 1 Wealth
Provides: Sanitary
Built Effect: +1 Morale

The Capital has Urban and an open slot.

The Empire has enough resources.

The Public Baths are built in the Capital.

Global Resource Pool becomes:

* 1 Labor
* 1 Material
* 2 Wealth

Morale increases from 4 to 5.

---

## Reveal 4

The fourth card is:

**Academy**
Type: Development — Structure
Requirement: Urban, Knowledge
Cost: 2 Wealth
Provides: Knowledge, Bureaucracy
Production: +1 Knowledge

The Empire has Urban, but it does not have Knowledge.

The Academy cannot be built.

No resources are spent.

The Academy enters the Stalled Row.

---

## Stalled Project Vote

Only Academy is in the Stalled Row.

Each player votes publicly.

Two players vote for Academy.

Academy becomes a Queued Project for the next Era.

# 26. Design Notes for Testing

During playtesting, track:

* how many Development cards are built per Era;
* how many cards enter the Stalled Row;
* how often Stalled Projects survive voting;
* how often Suspicion correctly constrains powerful players;
* whether players can infer motives without certainty;
* whether Ministries feel small but decisive;
* whether Plague and Unrest create pressure without excessive upkeep;
* whether Fortified is useful without being mandatory;
* whether Events feel disruptive but not purely destructive;
* how many Eras the Empire survives.

The core question for this prototype is:

**Does anonymous card commitment create enough bluffing, suspicion, and strategic steering to make shared empire-building fun?**
