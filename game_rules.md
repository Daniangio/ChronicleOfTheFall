# Chronicles of the Fall

## Official Prototype Rulebook — Updated Draft

A semi-cooperative game of imperial decline for 3–5 players.

---

# 1. Game Overview

In **Chronicles of the Fall**, players are members of a ruling council guiding a fragile Empire through expansion, crisis, disease, unrest, political compromise, and eventual collapse.

The Empire does not win. The Empire will fall.

Each player secretly represents a faction with a Hidden Agenda. Players must help the Empire survive long enough to shape it, but they are not trying to preserve the same future. When the Empire collapses, Hidden Agendas are revealed. A player wins if the final state of the Empire satisfies their Agenda.

The central mechanic of the game is the **Anonymous Council**. Each Era, players secretly commit cards into the machinery of government. These cards are revealed anonymously into the Council Docket, ordered by the Minister of the Empire, and resolved one at a time. Some cards build the Empire. Others disturb it. Since most contributors are anonymous, players must infer who is shaping the Empire, who is protecting it, and who is accelerating its fall.

---

# 2. Objective

The game ends immediately when any of the three Imperial Pillars reaches 0:

* **Treasury**
* **Stability**
* **Morale**

When the game ends, all players reveal their complete Hidden Agenda cards. Each
Agenda contains:

* a **Primary Legacy**, worth 4 points and mandatory;
* a **Secondary Legacy**, worth 2 points;
* a **Collapse Preference**, worth 2 points;
* a **Forbidden Future**, which is a veto rather than a scoring objective.

A player is eligible to win only if their Primary Legacy is satisfied, their
Forbidden Future is false, and they score at least 6 of the Agenda's 8 points.
Agenda conditions are evaluated after resolving the effect that caused a Pillar
to reach 0.

If multiple players are eligible, the player with the highest Agenda score wins.
If still tied, the tied player with the most total cards in hand and Scheme Slots
wins. If still tied, those players share the victory.

If no player satisfies their Hidden Agenda, all players lose.

---

# 3. Core Concepts

## 3.1 The Empire

The Empire is shared by all players.

It consists of:

* Cities
* Structures
* Global Resources
* Permanent Tags
* Condition Tokens
* Three Pillars: Treasury, Stability, Morale

No player owns a City or Structure unless a card explicitly says otherwise.

---

## 3.2 Pillars

The three Pillars measure the survival of the Empire.

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Treasury | Money, taxation, reserves, economic legitimacy |
| Stability | Political order, administrative control, institutional cohesion |
| Morale | Public hope, loyalty, social confidence |

Each Pillar starts at 5.

If any Pillar reaches 0, finish resolving the effect that caused the reduction. Then the game ends immediately.

---

## 3.3 Tags

Tags are permanent qualities of the Empire.

The prototype uses these Tags:

* **Culture**
* **Diplomacy**
* **Faith**
* **Industry**
* **Military**
* **Sanitary**
* **Science**

Tags are not resources. They are never spent.

Tags are used to:

* satisfy card requirements;
* resolve Event effects;
* determine Hidden Agenda conditions;
* protect against some Crisis Events;
* define the identity of Cities and Structures.

Unless a card says otherwise, a Structure provides its Tags as long as it remains built.

A Structure's Tag requirements are checked in the City where it would be built, unless the card marks the requirement as a **Global Requirement**.

---

## 3.4 Resources

Resources are temporary productive capacity generated by the Empire each Era.

Common resources:

* **Labor**
* **Food**
* **Favor**

Uncommon resources:

* **Material**
* **Influence**
* **Wealth**

Rare resources:

* **Knowledge**

Resources are global. No player owns them.

Resources are spent to build Development cards or resolve Event cards.

Unused resources are discarded during the Storage Phase unless stored by Cities or card effects.

---

## 3.5 Hidden Agenda Evaluation

Hidden Agendas are prebuilt complete cards. Players never assemble objectives
from separate modules during play.

Agenda conditions may evaluate:

* individual Tag counts or comparisons between Tags and Tag groups;
* current production of a Resource;
* Resource capacity, defined as current production plus stored Resources of that type;
* the collapsed Pillar or highest surviving Pillar;
* Plague, Global Unrest, and Fortified token counts;
* combined Tag and token counts;
* whether City Plague exceeds that City's Sanitary Tags;
* the number and maximum value of selected scoring Tags.

All conditions within one Agenda section must be satisfied together for that
section to be satisfied. A true Forbidden Future prevents victory regardless of
points.

The supported condition vocabulary and initial Agenda design pool are documented
in [agenda_system_spec.md](documentation/agenda_system_spec.md).

---

# 4. Card Types

There are two main card categories:

1. **Development cards**
2. **Event cards**

---

## 4.1 Development Cards

Development cards permanently change the Empire.

There are two Development subtypes:

### City

City cards create new City zones.

A City may have:

* Tags;
* Structure slots;
* production;
* storage;
* special rules.

Cities do not occupy Structure slots.

Example:

**Frontier Town**
Type: Development — City
Cost: 1 Labor, 1 Material
Slots: 3
Production: +1 Labor
Storage: 1

---

### Structure

Structure cards are built into Cities.

A Structure may have:

* Tag requirements;
* resource cost;
* Tags provided;
* production;
* built effects;
* ongoing effects;
* storage bonuses.

A Structure occupies one open slot in a City.

Unless marked as a **Global Requirement**, a Structure's Tag requirements must be satisfied by the City where it is placed.

Example:

**Public Baths**
Type: Development — Structure
Requirement: Sanitary 1 or Culture 1
Cost: 1 Labor, 1 Material, 1 Wealth
Provides: Sanitary
Built Effect: +1 Morale

---

## 4.2 Event Cards

Event cards resolve immediately when revealed in the Council Docket.

There are two Event types:

1. **Edict**
2. **Crisis**

When an Event finishes resolving, place an Edict in the Empire discard pile or
a Crisis in the Crisis discard pile unless the card says otherwise.

---

### Edict

Edicts are political decisions, reforms, emergency laws, or administrative maneuvers.

They may require resources and often change Pillars, rebalance resources, or modify rules temporarily.

Example:

**Emergency Taxation**
Type: Event — Edict
Cost: 1 Influence
Effect: Add 2 Wealth to the Global Resource Pool. Lose 1 Morale.

---

### Crisis

Crisis Events are severe player-triggered disasters or confrontations.

A Crisis usually checks Tags, tokens, or Pillars, then applies a success or failure effect.

Example:

**Border Raid**
Type: Event — Crisis
Check: Military 2
Success: Add 1 Wealth.
Failure: Lose 1 Stability.

Crisis cards are kept in a separate Crisis Deck and never shuffled into the
Empire Deck. Players receive Crisis cards at setup and during Crisis Intake.
They remain private in hand or Scheme Slots until committed.

Crisis cards cannot be discarded during Hand Reset. They may be moved, swapped,
and committed from Scheme Slots under the normal Plotting rules.

When a Crisis finishes resolving, place it face up in the Crisis discard pile.
It does not enter the Empire discard pile.

---

## 4.3 Event Costs and Requirements

If an Event has a cost and the cost cannot be paid when the Event resolves, the Event has no effect and is discarded face up unless the card says otherwise.

If an Event has a condition and that condition is not met, follow the card text. If no alternative effect is listed, discard the Event face up with no effect.

---

## 4.4 Event Resource Effects

A resource effect has a signed amount.

A positive amount adds resources to the Global Resource Pool.

A negative amount removes resources from the Global Resource Pool, without reducing any resource below zero.

The effect may name a specific resource. If it is general, the Minister of Health & Harvest chooses the affected resource. If that Ministry is vacant, the Minister of the Empire chooses.

---

## 4.5 Choice Ministers

An Event may name an optional **Choice Minister**.

If present, that Minister handles every choice created by that Event instead of the normal effect-specific Minister.

If the named Ministry is vacant, the Minister of the Empire decides.

If no Choice Minister is named, apply the normal decision rules for each effect.

---

## 4.6 Conditional Effects

A conditional effect may compare a Tag, resource, token count, or Pillar value with a fixed number.

A Tag condition may instead compare its count with the current count of another Tag, using greater-than, less-than, at-least, at-most, or equal operators.

---

## 4.7 Structure Requirement Waivers

An Event may grant a **Structure Requirement Waiver** for the current Era.

The next Structure built that Era may be missing at most one required Tag instance in its destination City.

The waiver is consumed automatically when that Structure is built, even if all of its Tag requirements were already satisfied.

A waiver cannot ignore resource costs, placement rules, or more than one missing Tag.

An unused waiver expires during the End-of-Era Cleanup Phase.

---

## 4.8 Resource Conversion

An Event may convert up to X units of one resource into the same number of another resource.

It converts the smaller of X and the amount currently available.

The source, destination, or both may be general. General resource types are chosen by the Event's Choice Minister, or by the Minister of Health & Harvest under the normal rules.

Source and destination must differ.

---

## 4.9 Draw Effects

An Event may create a **pending draw effect**.

Unless the card says otherwise, a draw effect means:

> The chosen player draws 1 additional card during the Hand Refill Phase of the current Era.

Examples:

* A normal player with one pending draw effect draws 4 cards instead of 3.
* The Minister of State with one pending draw effect draws 5 cards instead of 4.
* Multiple draw effects stack.

Draw effects do not cause immediate card draw during the Council Docket unless a card explicitly says so.

---

## 4.10 Reduced Refill Effects

An Event may make every player draw 1 fewer card during the Hand Refill Phase of
the current Era. Multiple reduced-refill effects stack, and a player can never
draw fewer than 0 cards.

Pending draw effects and reduced-refill effects are both applied when determining
the final number of cards drawn.

---

## 4.11 Event Token Effects

Event token effects use a signed amount for Plague, Unrest, or Fortified.

Positive values add tokens. Negative values remove tokens.

A token count can never fall below zero.

An Event may group several signed token changes into one City effect. The responsible Minister chooses one City, then every Plague, Unrest, and Fortified change in that grouped effect applies to that same City. The changes are not separate placement decisions.

The Event's Choice Minister makes this decision. If none is named, or if that Ministry is vacant, the Minister of the Empire decides.

---

## 4.12 Plague Suppression Effects

An Event may state that **Plague does not reduce Morale until the end of the Era**.

While this effect is active, Plague tokens remain on Cities and Plague checks still occur, but those checks cause no Morale loss.

The effect expires during the End-of-Era Cleanup Phase.

---

# 5. Discard Timing and Visibility

The Empire uses one face-up Empire discard pile and one face-up Crisis
discard pile.

Whenever a card is discarded, place it face up in its appropriate discard pile
immediately. Discard piles are public information and may be inspected, browsed,
and counted at any time.

Discard timing:

* Resolved Events are discarded immediately after they finish resolving.
* Development cards that fail to build are discarded immediately.
* Destroyed Structures are discarded immediately when destroyed.
* Non-Crisis cards remaining in players' hands are discarded during the Hand Reset Phase.
* Crisis cards remain in hand during the Hand Reset Phase.
* Cards in Scheme Slots are not discarded during the Hand Reset Phase.

When the Empire Deck is empty, shuffle the face-up Empire discard pile to form
a new Empire Deck. Crisis cards never enter this reshuffle.

---

# 6. Components

Each game uses:

* 1 Empire Deck
* 1 Crisis Deck
* 1 face-up Empire discard pile
* 1 face-up Crisis discard pile
* City cards
* Structure cards
* Event cards
* Hidden Agenda cards
* Ministry cards
* Minister of the Empire marker
* Suspicion tokens
* Plague tokens
* Unrest tokens
* Fortified tokens
* Resource tokens
* Pillar trackers
* 2 Scheme Slots for each player

---

# 7. Setup

## 7.1 Set Pillars

Set the Pillars to:

* Treasury: 5
* Stability: 5
* Morale: 5

---

## 7.2 Place the Capital

Place the Capital in play.

**Capital**
Type: City
Slots: 4
Production: 2 Labor
Storage: 2

The Capital may also have starting Tags if required by the current prototype deck. If no deck-specific setup is provided, the Capital starts with no specialized Tags.

---

## 7.3 Prepare the Empire Deck

Shuffle the Empire Deck.

The Empire Deck contains Development cards and Edict cards.

Crisis cards are removed from the Empire Deck and shuffled into the separate
Crisis Deck.

Recommended prototype deck composition:

* 40–45% Development cards
* 40–45% Edict cards
* 10–20% flexible political or advanced cards

---

## 7.4 Deal Hidden Agendas

Shuffle the deck of complete Hidden Agenda cards and deal 2 face down to each
player. Each player secretly keeps 1 as their Hidden Agenda. The unchosen Agenda
is placed face down into a sealed out-of-game discard pile that no player may
inspect.

Players keep their chosen Agenda secret until the Empire collapses. Optional
tabletop variants may instead deal 1 Agenda for faster setup, or deal 3 and let
each player keep 1 for greater choice. The current digital game implements the
standard two-card choice.

---

## 7.5 Assign Offices

Choose a random player to become the first **Minister of the Empire**.

Assign the four Ministries according to player count, following Section 8.

During the first Era, do not rotate offices. Begin with the Suspicion Phase if
the Level activates it in Era 1; otherwise begin with Production.

---

## 7.6 Deal Starting Hands

Each normal player receives a starting hand of 3 cards.

The initial Minister of State receives a starting hand of 4 cards.

For the first prototype, use one of these setup methods:

* **Simple setup:** deal all starting cards from the Empire Deck.
* **Guided setup:** give each player 2 random Base cards, then fill the rest of their starting hand from the Empire Deck.

Suggested Base cards include simple early Structures such as Farm, Chapel, Peasant Home, Woodcutter, Quarry, Shrine, Clinic, or Garrison.

After dealing starting hands, each player draws 1 card from the Crisis Deck and
adds it to their hand. If the Crisis Deck is empty, skip this draw.

---

## 7.7 Scheme Slots

Each player has 2 private Scheme Slots.

Cards in Scheme Slots:

* are private;
* remain in their Scheme Slots across Eras;
* do not count as cards in hand;
* may be committed during the Plotting Phase;
* may be moved back to hand during that player's Plotting turn;
* are discarded only when committed and resolved, left in hand during the Hand Reset Phase, or affected by a card effect.

Scheme Slots represent long-term plans, hidden preparations, delayed threats, or future projects.

A player may never have more than 2 Scheme cards unless a card explicitly changes this limit.

---

## 7.8 Hand Size

Each player has a normal hand size of 3 cards.

The Minister of State has a hand size of 4 cards for the current Era.

Cards in Scheme Slots do not count toward hand size.

Hands are temporary. Non-Crisis cards remaining in hand during the Hand Reset
Phase are discarded face up. Each player draws a new hand during the later Hand
Refill Phase. Crisis cards remain in hand.

---

# 8. Offices and Ministries

## 8.1 Minister of the Empire

The Minister of the Empire is the first player of the Era and the default decision-maker when another Ministry is vacant.

During the Council Docket Phase, the Minister of the Empire determines the order
in which committed cards resolve, subject to every Crisis resolving before every
non-Crisis card.

The Minister of the Empire cannot receive Suspicion and cannot be blocked or otherwise prevented from holding this office.

An effect that would block that player may still prevent them from holding another Ministry, but it cannot remove or skip their position as Minister of the Empire.

At the beginning of each new Era after the first, pass the office clockwise.

---

## 8.2 Ministries

There are four Ministries:

1. Minister of Cities
2. Minister of State
3. Minister of War
4. Minister of Health & Harvest

Ministries always remain in this order. There is no Ministry draft.

At the beginning of each new Era after the first, assignments advance so that the player who was Minister of State becomes Minister of War.

The Minister of State and Minister of War may never be held by the same player in the same Era.

---

## 8.3 Ministry Assignment by Player Count

### 3 Players

Assign all four Ministries in their fixed order around the players. One player holds two Ministries.

The shared pair must never be State and War.

### 4 Players

Assign one Ministry to each player in the fixed order.

### 5 Players

Assign the four Ministries in their fixed order. One player has no Ministry.

Advance all assignments in the same direction each Era.

---

## 8.4 Vacant Ministries

A Ministry may be vacant because:

* it is lost due to Suspicion;
* a card effect disables it;
* no player currently holds it.

If a vacant Ministry must make a decision, the Minister of the Empire makes that decision instead.

---

# 9. Ministry Powers

## 9.1 Minister of Cities

The Minister of Cities places all Cities and Structures.

When a Development card is successfully built, the Minister of Cities chooses its legal placement.

The Minister of Cities also chooses which leftover resources are stored if the Empire has more resources than storage capacity.

---

## 9.2 Minister of State

The Minister of State governs imperial legitimacy and political disorder.

When an effect places Unrest and does not specify whether it is City Unrest or Global Unrest, the Minister of State chooses.

If City Unrest is chosen, the Minister of State also chooses the City.

During the Hand Refill Phase, the Minister of State draws to a hand size of 4 instead of 3.

---

## 9.3 Minister of Health & Harvest

The Minister of Health & Harvest governs disease placement and food-health administration.

When an effect places Plague and does not specify a City, the Minister of Health & Harvest chooses the City.

When an effect gains a general resource without specifying its type, the Minister
of Health & Harvest chooses the resource gained.

When an effect converts up to a stated number of resources, the Minister of
Health & Harvest chooses the source and destination when either is general and
chooses any conversion amount from 0 up to the stated limit, subject to available
resources.

---

## 9.4 Minister of War

The Minister of War resolves revolts.

When Global Unrest reaches its threshold, the Minister of War chooses how the Imperial Unrest Crisis is resolved.

When a City reaches 2 Unrest, the Minister of War chooses how that revolt is resolved.

Whenever an effect destroys Structures in Cities, the Minister of War chooses
the eligible Structures that are destroyed.

When an effect places Fortified and does not specify a City, the Minister of War chooses the City.

The Minister of War usually makes choices for military Crises unless the card
names another Choice Minister.

---

# 10. Era Sequence

Each Era follows this sequence:

1. **Office Rotation Phase**
2. **Suspicion Phase**, once activated by the Level
3. **Production Phase**
4. **Plotting Phase**
5. **Hand Reset Phase**
6. **Council Docket Phase**
7. **Condition Phase**
8. **Storage Phase**
9. **Crisis Intake Phase**
10. **Hand Refill Phase**
11. **End-of-Era Cleanup Phase**

During the first Era, skip the Office Rotation Phase. Skip Suspicion as well
unless the Level activates it in Era 1.

---

# 11. Office Rotation Phase

At the beginning of each Era after the first:

1. Pass the Minister of the Empire marker clockwise.
2. Advance Ministry assignments according to the fixed Ministry order.
3. Confirm that no player holds both Minister of State and Minister of War.
4. Resolve any office-related effects that occur at the beginning of the Era.

Then proceed to the Suspicion Phase if it is active, or Production otherwise.

---

# 12. Suspicion Phase

Suspicion represents public blame, court rumors, and political distrust.

Each Level defines the first Era in which Suspicion is active. The default is
Era 5. Before that Era, skip this phase.

Once Suspicion is active, each player places one Suspicion token at the beginning
of every Era after offices are set.

Starting with the Minister of the Empire and proceeding clockwise, each player places their Suspicion token on:

* another player;
* or no one.

A player may not place Suspicion on themselves.

A player may not place Suspicion on the Minister of the Empire.

Suspicion tokens are public.

---

## 12.1 Suspicion Effects

After all Suspicion tokens are placed, count how many each player received.

### 0–1 Suspicion

No effect.

### 2 Suspicion

The player must commit their card face up during the Plotting Phase.

In a three-player game, 2 Suspicion also causes every effect listed under
**3+ Suspicion**.

### 3+ Suspicion

The player must commit face up and suffers these additional effects for this Era:

1. They lose every Ministry they hold for this Era.
2. They cannot commit an Event card.

A lost Ministry becomes vacant until the end of the Era. If that Ministry must
make a decision, the Minister of the Empire decides instead.

If the player has only Event cards across their hand and Scheme Slots, they reveal
all those cards and commit nothing.

---

# 13. Production Phase

During Production, the Empire produces resources.
Production resolves automatically as soon as the phase begins.

Add to the Global Resource Pool:

* production from all Cities;
* production from all Structures;
* stored resources from previous Eras.

Stored resources are moved from Cities into the Global Resource Pool during Production.

Resources in the Global Resource Pool are shared by the Empire and owned by no player.

Example:

The Empire has:

* Capital: 2 Labor
* Quarry: +1 Material
* Market Hall: +1 Wealth

The Global Resource Pool becomes:

* 2 Labor
* 1 Material
* 1 Wealth

---

# 14. Plotting Phase

Each player must commit 1 card if able.

A player may commit:

* 1 card from hand;
* or 1 card from a Scheme Slot.

Players perform Plotting in parallel. There is no active-player order during this
phase. Once a player commits, that player has completed Plotting for the Era.

---

## 14.1 Scheming During Plotting

Before committing, each player may rearrange cards between their hand and Scheme Slots.

The player may:

* move any number of cards from hand into empty Scheme Slots;
* move any number of cards from Scheme Slots back into hand;
* replace one or both Scheme cards with cards from hand.

At all times, the player may have no more than 2 cards in Scheme Slots.

After Scheming, the player commits 1 card from either hand or Scheme, if able.

Committing a card ends that player's Plotting turn.

A committed Scheme card leaves its Scheme Slot.

---

## 14.2 No Voluntary Discard During Plotting

Players may not voluntarily discard cards during Plotting.

Cards are discarded only when:

* a card effect discards them;
* a public card or Structure is discarded by the rules;
* cards remaining in hand are discarded during the Hand Reset Phase.

---

## 14.3 Anonymous Commitments

Cards are normally committed face down.

All face-down committed cards are collected without revealing who contributed each card.

Players should not know which player submitted which face-down card.

---

## 14.4 Face-Up Committed Cards

A player with 2+ Suspicion must commit face up.

If a player must commit face up, the card remains visibly associated with that player.

For physical play:

* place the real card face up in front of that player;
* place a face-down placeholder among the commitments;
* when commitments enter the Council Docket, replace that placeholder with the associated face-up card.

For digital play, the Docket shows that the card belongs to the suspected player.

---

# 15. Hand Reset Phase

After every player has committed or declared that they cannot commit, each player
discards all non-Crisis cards remaining in hand face up into the Empire discard
pile.

Crisis cards remain in hand.

Cards in Scheme Slots are not discarded.

After Hand Reset, reveal the committed cards into the Council Docket.

---

# 16. Council Docket Phase

After every player has committed or declared that they cannot commit:

1. Reveal all committed cards into the **Council Docket**.
2. Keep each normal contributor anonymous.
3. Keep any face-up Suspicion commitments associated with their contributor.
4. Move every Crisis to the front of the Docket.
5. The Minister of the Empire orders the Crisis cards among themselves, then
   orders all remaining cards among themselves.
6. Lock the chosen order.
7. Resolve the first card, then continue through the Docket from left to right.

The Docket is not shuffled and its resolution order is not random.

Every Crisis must finish resolving before any non-Crisis card can resolve. A
Crisis may never be ordered behind an Edict or Development card.

Resolve each card fully before resolving the next card.

If any effect changes the Global Resource Pool, that change applies immediately and may affect cards later in the Docket.

If any effect causes a Pillar to reach 0, finish resolving that card, then the game ends immediately.

---

# 17. Resolving Development Cards

When a Development card is resolved, check whether it can be built.

A Development card can be built only if:

1. all Tag requirements are satisfied;
2. all resource costs are available in the Global Resource Pool;
3. a legal City or placement target exists;
4. a Structure has an available slot in the chosen City.

If all conditions are satisfied, the card is built.

If even one condition is not satisfied, the card is not built and is discarded
face up immediately.

---

## 17.1 Resource Consumption

Resource payment is all-or-nothing.

If the full cost is available, spend the full cost and build the card.

If even one required resource is missing, spend nothing and discard the card
face up immediately.

---

## 17.2 Structure Placement

When a Structure is successfully built, the Minister of Cities chooses the City where it is placed.

The chosen City must:

* satisfy the Structure's requirements;
* have an open slot;
* obey all card-specific placement rules.

If no legal City exists, discard the Structure face up immediately.

---

## 17.3 City Placement

When a City card is successfully built, the Minister of Cities places it according to the card text.

If the card does not specify restrictions, the City enters play as a new City zone.

---

# 18. Resolving Event Cards

Event cards resolve immediately when revealed in the Council Docket.

If an Event has a cost and the cost cannot be paid, the Event has no effect and is discarded face up unless the card says otherwise.

If an Event has a condition and the condition is not met, follow the card text. If no alternative effect is listed, discard it face up with no effect.

When an Event finishes resolving, discard it face up unless a card says otherwise.

---

# 19. Condition Tokens

Condition Tokens represent persistent local or global states affecting the Empire.

The prototype uses three Condition Tokens:

* Plague
* Unrest
* Fortified

Plague and Unrest are negative tokens.

Fortified is a positive token.

---

# 20. Plague

Plague represents disease, contamination, famine spillover, or public health collapse.

Plague is placed on Cities.

When an effect places Plague and does not specify a City, the Minister of Health & Harvest chooses the City.

---

## 20.1 Plague Check

During the Condition Phase, check each City separately.

If a City has more Plague tokens than Sanitary Tags, lose 1 Morale.

Then leave the Plague tokens in place.

If an effect prevents Plague from reducing Morale for the current Era, perform the check but do not lose Morale. The Plague tokens remain in place.

Example:

The Capital has:

* 2 Plague tokens
* 1 Sanitary Tag

Because Plague is greater than Sanitary, the Empire loses 1 Morale.

If the Capital later has:

* 2 Plague tokens
* 2 Sanitary Tags

there is no Morale loss from Plague in that City.

---

## 20.2 Removing Plague

Cards may remove Plague.

Example:

**Clinic**
Built Effect: Remove up to 1 Plague from this City.

**Hospital**
Built Effect: Remove up to 2 Plague from this City.

**Quarantine Edict**
Type: Event — Edict
Effect: Remove up to 2 Plague from one City. Then place 1 Unrest on that City.

---

# 21. Unrest

Unrest represents revolt, political disorder, social anger, and collapse of legitimacy.

Unrest may be placed:

* on a City;
* or globally on the Empire.

When an effect places Unrest and does not specify City or Global, the Minister of State chooses whether to place it globally or on a City.

If City Unrest is chosen and the effect does not specify a City, the Minister of State chooses the City.

---

## 21.1 City Unrest

City Unrest is placed on Cities.

When a City reaches 2 Unrest tokens, a Revolt immediately triggers in that City.

The Minister of War chooses one resolution:

### Suppress the Revolt

Remove all Unrest from that City.
Lose 2 Morale.

### Buy Peace

Remove all Unrest from that City.
Lose 2 Treasury.

### Let It Burn

Remove all Unrest from that City.
Destroy 2 Structures in that City, chosen by the Minister of War.

Destroyed Structures are discarded face up immediately.

If fewer than 2 Structures are present, destroy as many as possible.

Cities themselves are not destroyed by Revolt unless a card says otherwise.

---

## 21.2 Global Unrest

Global Unrest is placed on the Empire.

When the Empire reaches 3 Global Unrest tokens, an Imperial Unrest Crisis immediately triggers.

The Minister of War chooses one resolution:

### Repression

Remove all Global Unrest.
Lose 3 Morale.
Gain 1 Stability.

### Concessions

Remove all Global Unrest.
Lose 3 Treasury.
Gain 1 Morale.

### Fragmentation

Remove all Global Unrest.
Lose 3 Stability.
Gain 1 Treasury.

---

## 21.3 Timing of Unrest

Unrest thresholds resolve immediately.

If an Event places the second Unrest on a City, resolve the City Revolt before continuing with the Event if possible.

If an Event places the third Global Unrest token, resolve the Imperial Unrest Crisis immediately before revealing the next card.

---

# 22. Fortified

Fortified represents walls, guard networks, military readiness, or protected infrastructure.

Fortified is placed on Cities.

A City may have at most 1 Fortified token.

A Fortified City contributes +1 Military Tag.

If a Structure in a Fortified City would be destroyed, remove the Fortified token instead of destroying one Structure.

If an effect would destroy 2 Structures in a Fortified City, Fortified prevents one destruction. The other destruction still occurs if a valid Structure remains.

Example:

A City Revolt resolves with Let It Burn in a Fortified City containing three Structures.

The Minister of War chooses two Structures to destroy.

Fortified is removed to prevent one of those destructions.

The other chosen Structure is destroyed and discarded face up immediately.

---

# 23. Condition Phase

During the Condition Phase:

1. Resolve all Plague checks.
2. Apply any other effects that explicitly occur during the Condition Phase.

Unrest does not wait until the Condition Phase. Unrest resolves immediately when its threshold is reached.

Fortified has no Condition Phase effect.

---

# 24. Storage Phase

After the Condition Phase, unused resources in the Global Resource Pool are discarded unless stored.

Each City has storage capacity.

The Capital has Storage 2 by default.

Other Cities and Structures may add storage.

If there are more leftover resources than available storage, the Minister of Cities chooses which resources are stored.

Stored resources are placed on Cities.

All unstored resources are discarded from the Global Resource Pool.

During the next Production Phase, all stored resources are moved into the Global Resource Pool.

Example:

The Empire has leftover:

* 1 Labor
* 1 Material
* 2 Wealth

The Capital has Storage 2.

The Minister of Cities chooses to store:

* 1 Material
* 1 Wealth

The remaining Labor and Wealth are discarded.

---

# 25. Crisis Intake Phase

After Storage and before Hand Refill, check the completed Era number.

If it is divisible by 5, each player draws 1 card from the Crisis Deck and adds
it to their hand. This occurs at the end of Eras 5, 10, 15, 20, and so on.

If the Crisis Deck is empty, skip any draw it cannot supply.

---

# 26. Hand Refill Phase

Each player draws a new hand.

* Normal players draw 3 cards.
* The Minister of State draws 4 cards.

If a player has one or more pending draw effects, they draw 1 additional card for each pending draw effect.

Subtract 1 card for each active reduced-refill effect, to a minimum of 0.

After drawing, clear all pending draw effects.

Cards in Scheme Slots remain in place for the next Era.

Players may not move cards into Scheme Slots during the Hand Refill Phase unless a card explicitly says so.

Scheming happens during the Plotting Phase.

---

# 27. End-of-Era Cleanup Phase

During the End-of-Era Cleanup Phase:

1. Remove all Suspicion tokens.
2. End all effects that last "until the end of the Era".
3. Clear any temporary waivers or unresolved temporary modifiers.
4. If the Empire Deck is empty, shuffle the face-up discard pile to form a new Empire Deck.

Do not move resolved Events, destroyed Structures, or failed Developments during
this phase. Those cards are discarded immediately when they resolve, are
destroyed, or fail to build.

Then the Era ends.

---

# 28. Design Notes for Testing

During playtesting, track:

* how many Development cards are built per Era;
* how often Suspicion correctly constrains powerful players;
* whether players can infer motives without certainty;
* whether Ministries feel small but decisive;
* whether Plague and Unrest create pressure without excessive upkeep;
* whether Fortified is useful without being mandatory;
* whether Events feel disruptive but not purely destructive;
* how many Eras the Empire survives;
* how often Scheme Slots are used to preserve meaningful long-term plans;
* how often the Minister of the Empire's Docket order meaningfully changes outcomes.

The core question for this prototype is:

**Does anonymous card commitment create enough bluffing, suspicion, and strategic steering to make shared empire-building fun?**
